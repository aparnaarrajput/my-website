About Me

Mathematician & Researcher

I am a mathematician with a PhD in Mathematics from Concordia University. My research background is in dynamical systems, ergodic theory, and transfer operator theory. My doctoral research focused on the spectral properties and quasi-compactness of the Frobenius–Perron operator for classes of interval maps.

I am currently expanding my research toward data-driven methods for complex systems. I am interested in connecting rigorous operator-theoretic ideas with finite and noisy observations, machine learning, and anomaly detection. My goal is to develop mathematically grounded approaches for learning and detecting changes in underlying dynamical behavior from data.

Focus Areas

Research Interests

Ergodic Theory

Studying the long-term statistical behavior of dynamical systems, including ergodicity, mixing properties, and the existence of invariant measures.

Dynamical Systems

Analyzing the evolution of interval maps, piecewise expanding maps, and piecewise convex maps with finitely or countably many branches.

Transfer Operators

Investigating the spectral theory of the Frobenius–Perron operator, with emphasis on quasi-compactness and the structure of the essential spectrum.

Absolutely Continuous Invariant Measures

Establishing existence and uniqueness of ACIMs for broad classes of non-uniformly expanding maps using functional-analytic methods.

Statistical Modeling

Exploring the connections between classical ergodic results and modern probabilistic and data-driven modeling frameworks.

Functional Analysis

Applying tools from Banach space theory, spectral theory, and operator algebras to problems in mathematical physics and probability theory.

Operator-Based Anomaly Detection

A research-oriented Python project that develops an operator-theoretic approach to anomaly detection in time-series data. The method estimates empirical transition operators from observations and detects changes in underlying dynamics through changes in estimated invariant distributions.
